Echo World

An interactive world that remembers.

A game-design concept built around a world that reacts to what you do — where characters remember your choices, narratives branch, and reputation shapes what happens next.

No build yet — concept stage
Echo World.Concept identity — no running build exists to screenshot
Honest status

Echo World is a design concept. There is no implemented game, no engine code and no prototype build. Everything below describes the intended design and the systems I'm working through on paper — not shipped functionality.

01 — The ideaA world that reacts to what you did

Most games reset. You make choices, but the world rarely carries them — a character who betrayed you greets you the same way in the next playthrough, and the story forgets. Echo World starts from the opposite instinct: the world should remember.

The vision is a game where decisions genuinely propagate. Help a faction and its members trust you; rob a merchant and word spreads; break a promise and a character's dialogue changes for the rest of the game. The fun comes from discovering how your own history reshapes the experience — and from wanting to replay it a different way to see what else could have happened.

02 — Core concepts

  • Interactive storytelling — the player drives the narrative rather than watching it.
  • Branching narratives — choices fork the story into genuinely different outcomes.
  • Reactive characters — NPCs whose behaviour and dialogue reflect their history with you.
  • Reputation systems — standing with people and factions that opens or closes opportunities.
  • Persistent decision tracking — a record of what you did that the game reads from.
  • Adaptive dialogue — conversations that shift based on context and your past actions.

03 — Who it's for

Players who care about story and consequence — people who enjoy role-playing games, immersive sims and narrative adventures, and who replay games specifically to explore different choices. It's aimed at an audience that values being remembered by the world they're inside.

05 — The engineering problem I find interesting

Designing a world that "remembers" is fundamentally a state-modelling problem. Every action needs to write to some persistent record, and the game has to read that record to decide how the world responds — without the state space exploding or the story becoming incoherent. The interesting questions are: how granular should a decision be, how do you let consequences cascade without writing every branch by hand, and how do you keep a player's history legible to them so their choices feel meaningful rather than arbitrary.

That's the part I want to build first: a small, honest decision-and-consequence engine I can test with a handful of scenarios before any world-building happens around it.

06 — Next steps

The next real step is to build the foundational systems in isolation — decision tracking, a reputation model, and a small branching scenario — and prove the loop works in a minimal prototype before committing to a full game. Nothing above is implemented yet; I'm documenting the concept so the intent is clear and so I can hold myself to building it properly.
