Future You

Explore your possibilities.

An interactive personal-planning concept — set goals, compare alternative paths, and watch how different choices might shape your progress over time. Reflection and planning, not prediction.

No build yet — concept stage

Honest status

Future You is a design concept. There is no implemented application, no scenario engine and no working prototype. Everything below describes the intended product and the design problems I'm working through — not shipped functionality. It deliberately does not claim to predict anyone's future.

01 — The ideaMake planning visual and comparable

Goal-setting tools usually just store a target. They don't help you think about how you'll get there, or what changes if you take a different route. People make better decisions when they can see alternatives side by side rather than committing to one path blind.

Future You imagines defining an objective, then exploring different approaches to it — studying while working, saving aggressively versus investing, learning one skill versus several — and visualising how each path might play out over a timeline. The aim is reflection: helping you make a more informed choice, not forecasting a certain outcome.

02 — Core concepts

  • Goal setting — defining the objective you actually want to work toward.
  • Scenario simulation — comparing alternative approaches and their assumptions.
  • Interactive timelines — seeing a path unfold over time rather than as a static list.
  • Progress visualisation — tracking how far along each pathway you are.
  • Personalised planning — tooling that adapts to your specific situation and constraints.
  • AI-assisted suggestions — recommendations to help consider options you might have missed.

03 — Who it's for

People at a genuine decision point — students weighing study and work, early-career developers choosing what to learn, anyone planning finances, a business or a move. It's for those who want a structured way to think through options rather than a motivational app that just records a resolution.

05 — The design problem I find interesting

The trap with a product like this is implying false precision. A simulation that shows a confident curve for "where you'll be in five years" is dishonest, because the future isn't predictable. So the interesting design problem is: how do you make scenario comparison genuinely useful while keeping it clearly framed as exploration? My instinct is to show relative differences between paths under stated assumptions, rather than absolute predictions — so the tool helps you reason about trade-offs without pretending to know the outcome.

Under the hood that means designing a scenario model that is transparent about its assumptions and simple enough to trust, rather than a black box that spits out numbers.

06 — Next steps

The next step would be to design the scenario engine on paper first — what a "pathway" consists of, how assumptions are recorded and surfaced, and how two paths get compared honestly — before building any interface. Nothing is implemented yet; this is a documented concept with its limits stated up front.
