Reality Lens

Turn what you see into what you understand.

A camera-first discovery and learning concept — point your phone at the world around you and get recognition, a clear explanation, translation, and follow-up questions to keep learning.

No build yet — concept stage
Reality Lens.Concept identity — no running build exists to screenshot
Honest status

Reality Lens is a design concept. There is no implemented app, no computer-vision code and no working camera integration. What follows describes the intended experience and the problems I'd need to solve — not shipped functionality. It is not an existing AR product.

01 — The ideaCuriosity answered at the point of seeing

People encounter things they don't understand constantly — a plant on a walk, a machine in a factory, a sign in a language they can't read. Today you have to type a description into a search box, which is clumsy and often wrong. The camera is a faster, more natural input: you're already looking at the thing.

Reality Lens imagines opening your camera, pointing it at something, and immediately getting what it is, a plain-language explanation of how it works, a translation if text is present, and a few suggested follow-up questions so one glance can turn into genuine understanding.

02 — Core concepts

  • Computer vision & image recognition — identifying what the camera is pointed at.
  • Contextual explanations — not just a label, but a useful description of what it is and how it works.
  • Text translation — reading and translating signage and text found in view.
  • Interactive follow-up questions — suggested next questions that deepen understanding.
  • Conversational assistance — asking further questions about what you're looking at.
  • Camera-first interface — the viewfinder is the primary input, not a text box.

03 — Who it's for

Curious people in the moment of not knowing — students and lifelong learners, travellers reading foreign signs, hobbyists identifying plants or parts, and anyone who wants to understand the everyday world around them without stopping to type a description into a search engine.

04 — What exists today

Designed on paper

  • Core camera-first concept
  • Experience flow outlined
  • Recognition → explain → ask loop

In exploration

  • Which vision services fit the job
  • Accuracy vs latency trade-offs
  • Mobile UX for the capture flow

Not started

  • App implementation
  • Camera & vision integration
  • Translation pipeline

05 — The hard parts, honestly

The concept is easy to describe and hard to make genuinely useful. The real engineering challenges are recognition accuracy — a wrong label is worse than no label — and latency, because a camera app that takes ten seconds to answer has already lost the moment. There's also the integration question: whether to use a hosted vision API, run a model on-device for speed and privacy, or combine both. I'd want to establish a reliable recognition-and-explanation loop with a narrow, well-understood scope before promising it works on anything the camera sees.

06 — Next steps

The first real milestone would be a narrow prototype: recognise a limited, well-defined category of object, return an accurate explanation, and make the capture-to-answer flow feel instant. Only once that loop is trustworthy would it make sense to widen recognition and add translation. Nothing is implemented yet — this is a documented concept, honestly labelled.
